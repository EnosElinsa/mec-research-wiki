# HAPS Communication Networks: A Tutorial-cum-Survey on Integration with Optical Atmospheric Sensing

## Citation

Elkhazraji, A., Alouini, M.-S., & Farooq, A. *HAPS Communication Networks: A Tutorial-cum-Survey on Integration with Optical Atmospheric Sensing*. Venue, year, and DOI are not in the parse.

## TL;DR

A tutorial and survey on HAPS free-space optical (FSO) backhaul and laser-based atmospheric sensing sharing one optical payload. It identifies differential absorption lidar (DIAL) in the telecom C-band as the most promising sensing modality and lays out a roadmap for co-locating DIAL with multi-Gbps FSO links on balloons, fixed-wing aircraft, and airships.

## Related Work Paragraph

> Ready to reuse in a literature review. Replace `[x]` with the formal citation number.

Elkhazraji et al. [x] reviewed recent developments in HAPS-based optical communication and provided a tutorial on integrating it with atmospheric remote sensing through shared optical links. They assessed multiple sensing modalities suitable for HAPS deployment and identified differential absorption lidar as the most promising because of its range-resolved sensitivity, spectral selectivity, and compatibility with HAPS payload constraints. The article presents a technical roadmap for telecom-band differential absorption lidar on HAPS in coexistence with high-throughput free-space optical communication, focusing on the C-band from 1.53 to 1.57 micrometers. It also examines the architectural, spectral, and atmospheric advantages of HAPS over terrestrial and satellite systems, categorizes balloon, fixed-wing, and airship architectures, and discusses platform constraints and use cases. The analyses indicate viable spectral windows that support both high-throughput data transfer and sensitive retrieval of trace gases such as carbon dioxide and methane.

## Scope and method

The article combines a Scopus-based bibliometric review, link-geometry and optical-propagation analysis, and spectral simulations. It does not define an optimization or control model.

## Key findings

- HAPS between 17 and 22 km occupy a favorable regime for co-deploying optical communication and sensing payloads.
- Shared hardware and spectrum can support both FSO backhaul and DIAL sensing in the telecom C-band.
- Platform-level limits on power, weight, and stability shape which architectures fit.

## Limitations / future work

The authors identify integration, payload, and operational challenges that remain before deployment.
