# Unleashing the Potential of Networked Tethered Flying Platforms: Prospects, Challenges, and Applications

## Citation

Belmekki, B. E. Y., & Alouini, M.-S. (2022). *Unleashing the Potential of Networked Tethered Flying Platforms: Prospects, Challenges, and Applications*. **IEEE Open Journal of Vehicular Technology, 3**. DOI: 10.1109/OJVT.2022.3177946.

## TL;DR

A survey of networked tethered flying platforms (NTFPs): aerial platforms such as tethered drones and aerostats linked to ground stations by tethers that supply continuous power and data. It reviews platform types, regulation, and case studies, then their role in 6G non-terrestrial networks, system and channel models, enabling technologies, economics, and open problems.

## Related Work Paragraph

Belmekki and Alouini [x] surveyed networked tethered flying platforms, aerial platforms linked to ground stations through tethers that provide continuous power and data. They reviewed the existing types of these platforms, including their components, characteristics, applications, advantages, and regulations, and described case studies in several fields. The survey then investigated their role in wireless communications and their integration into non-terrestrial networks alongside free-flying platforms and satellites in 6G, presenting system models, performance studies, channel models, and the integration of technologies such as RIS, NOMA, mmWave and THz, FSO, VLC, LPWAN, and massive MIMO. An economic analysis indicates, for example, that one tethered platform at 250 m can match the coverage of 16 cell towers at about half the cost. The article closes with challenges and open problems.

## Scope and method

The survey combines literature review, Earth-geometry coverage analysis for tethered platforms, case studies of companies and deployments, and a cost comparison. It does not define a reusable optimization or control model.

## Key findings

- Tethers trade mobility for endurance, broad coverage, and robust backhaul.
- One reported deployment replaces 15 cell towers and reduces costs by 60%.
- Compared with 16 cell towers, an NTFP at 250 m has 90% lower CAPEX and 30% lower OPEX in the authors' analysis.

## Limitations / future work

Open problems include tether management, regulation, channel modeling, and integration with other NTN layers.
