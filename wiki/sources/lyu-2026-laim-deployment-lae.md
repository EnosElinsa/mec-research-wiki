# Empowering Intelligent Low-Altitude Economy With Large AI Model Deployment

## Citation

Lyu, Z., Gao, Y., Chen, J., Du, H., Xu, J., Huang, K., & Kim, D. I. (2026). *Empowering Intelligent Low-Altitude Economy With Large AI Model Deployment*. **IEEE Wireless Communications**. DOI: 10.1109/MWC.2025.3631765.

## TL;DR

A magazine article on deploying large AI models (LAIMs) in the low-altitude economy. It proposes a hierarchical architecture, enabling techniques for LAIMs and low-altitude systems to co-evolve, and a task-oriented execution pipeline, then illustrates them with real-world case studies and open challenges.

## Related Work Paragraph

> Ready to reuse in a literature review. Replace `[x]` with the formal citation number.

Lyu et al. [x] examined how large artificial intelligence models can enhance the intelligence of low-altitude economy services. They identified challenges including the gap between model computation and storage demands and the limited onboard resources of low-altitude entities, the mismatch between lab-trained models and dynamic physical environments, and the inefficiency of decoupled sensing, communication, and computation designs. To address them, they proposed a hierarchical system architecture for model deployment and presented representative application scenarios. They explored enabling techniques for the mutual co-evolution of large models and low-altitude systems and introduced a task-oriented execution pipeline for scalable and adaptive service delivery. The framework is validated through real-world case studies, and open challenges such as fusing airspace knowledge graphs with large models are outlined.

## Scope and method

The article combines architecture, technique overview, and case studies, without a formal decision model.

## Key findings

- A hierarchical architecture spreads LAIM workloads across low-altitude and ground tiers.
- Co-evolution techniques adapt lab-trained models to changing environments.
- Case studies support the task-oriented pipeline.

## Limitations / future work

Open directions include airspace knowledge graphs with LAIMs and other items listed by the authors.
