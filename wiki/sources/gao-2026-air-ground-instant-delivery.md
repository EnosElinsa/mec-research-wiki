# Cooperative Air-Ground Instant Delivery by UAVs and Crowdsourced Taxis: Joint UAV Station Deployment and Delivery Scheduling

## Citation

Gao, J., Wang, Q., Zhang, X., Shi, J., Zhao, X., Liang, Y., Guo, B., Han, Q., & Pan, Y. (2026). *Cooperative Air-Ground Instant Delivery by UAVs and Crowdsourced Taxis: Joint UAV Station Deployment and Delivery Scheduling*. **IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing**. DOI: 10.1109/TMC.2025.3634430.

## TL;DR

Builds a city-scale instant-delivery system that pairs UAVs with crowdsourced taxis. It places UAV stations from gaps between demand and taxi capacity, predicts demand to reposition UAVs, transfers human-courier assignment preferences to UAV/taxi models, and combines preference-first assignment with cost-aware generalized assignment.

## Problem

Taxis are limited by passenger routes, allowable detours, timing, and participation, while UAVs have finite range, battery, payload, and no-fly-zone constraints. Demand and taxi availability vary across space and time, so either delivery mode alone leaves avoidable service gaps.

## System and data model

- Taxis serve route-compatible, longer, or no-fly-zone parcels; UAVs serve demand poorly matched to taxi capacity.
- Taxi movement is modeled in one-minute slots over 1 km grids with origin-destination, halfway, and unoccupied delivery modes.
- UAV energy depends on distance, payload, and wind, with reserve-energy and deadline constraints.
- The evaluation uses one month of Shanghai courier, parcel, taxi, weather, and regional-function traces: at least 802,000 instant-delivery order records generated by more than 31,000 couriers, plus 3.4 billion trajectory records from more than 13,000 taxis over 6,400 square kilometers.

## Method

Weighted K-Means++-style clustering places stations according to historical delivery gaps. An MLP predicts grid-level demand from weather and region functions; a heuristic repositions UAVs toward predicted unmet demand. A courier-preference MLP is transferred and fine-tuned for UAV and taxi choices using simulated mode-specific labels. The scheduler first maximizes learned assignment preference, then sends remaining parcels to a generalized assignment solver that balances delivery count and cost.

## Key findings

- The first 23 days train the models and the final seven days evaluate them; reported results are seven-day means with standard-deviation error bars.
- At 100% demand, UT+ delivers more than 20,800 parcels: 27.4% above UT, 116.9% above UAV-only, and 50.1% above taxi-only. It is 19.2% cheaper and causes 36.3% less passenger impact than UT in the stated comparison.
- Taxi-capacity adjustment reduces time by 26.6% and cost by 24.2% relative to the unadjusted variant. Omitting UAV repositioning raises time by 14.6% and cost by 16.7%.
- With only 5% taxi participation, UT+ completes 91.2% of parcels. At 20% participation it delivers more than 21,400 parcels and saves 32.9% against UT and 62.7% against taxi-only in the reported cost comparison.
- The evaluation assumes at most one parcel per taxi, 40 minutes of unloaded UAV endurance, and parcel weights from 0.2 to 1.0 kg.

## Limitations / parse caveats

The evaluation is trace-driven but mode-specific UAV/taxi transfer-learning labels and counterfactual deliveries are simulated; it is not a live delivery deployment. Several equations and parameter arrays are corrupted or incomplete, including the full DDL/PTL sweeps and UAV speed. Table I reports at least 8,000 weather records while prose says more than 1,400. A 2024 ICDE paper with a shorter title appears in the references and is not this journal article. The local parse lacks publication metadata; the exact-title TMC record was verified through Crossref.
