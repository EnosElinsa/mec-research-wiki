3. Map the main research clusters, recurring system models, decision variables, objectives, constraints, assumptions, limitations, and stated future work.
4. Generate at least three substantially different candidate ideas internally. For each candidate, test:
   - the concrete operational problem and why it matters;
   - the closest existing work and the remaining technical problem;
   - whether the novelty is a real change in state, lifecycle, resource relationship, timescale, uncertainty, safety, reliability, or service continuity;
   - whether the idea is more than replacing one optimizer or DRL algorithm with another;

## Paper-writing and citation rules

Write the main body as a formal Chinese journal manuscript rather than as a research note. Match the paragraph logic, terminology density, and symbol-first pacing of the designated reference papers when the user provides them.

The introduction must establish two connected causal chains:

1. application motivation -> limitations of the closest operating paradigm -> concrete proposed scenario and operational process -> formal research problem;
2. scenario-specific decision structure -> why a generic solver is inadequate -> algorithm modules designed for those exact structural difficulties.

Do not begin the algorithm paragraph by merely stating that DRL or another solver is popular. Explain why online learning or optimization is needed, which scenario state makes the problem sequential, which variables are discrete or continuous, how current decisions change future feasibility, and how each proposed algorithm component addresses one identified difficulty. Treat the optimizer backbone as an implementation vehicle, not as the innovation itself.

The related-work section must follow the gap structure established in the introduction without repeating the introduction. The introduction states category-level motivation and limitations; related work provides paper-level evidence. Organize related work by mechanisms that correspond to the introduction, and make every subsection follow the sequence `representative work -> what it solves -> the precise assumption or boundary that remains`. End each subsection with a bounded synthesis that leads back to the paper's problem.

Build the network model by specializing and extending the closest established architecture whenever possible. Cite the architectural source, state which entities, time model, channels, and controller are inherited, and state which deployment, association, trajectory, or routing decisions are supplied by an upper layer or held fixed. Add only the operational states, modules, variables, and constraints required by the proposed scenario. Do not redesign unrelated layers or present inherited components as contributions.

Use citations at the point of the supported claim. Prefer recent and directly relevant papers, while retaining older foundational work only when it defines a method or concept that newer papers still rely on. Avoid using the same small group of citations for unrelated claims. Do not write citations as `Author et al. in *Full Paper Title* [x]`; use normal paper prose such as `Author et al. studied ... [x]`. Do not place local repository paths in the main paper. Put retrieval paths, novelty boundaries, and implementation audit details in an appendix when they remain useful.

Format references in IEEE Transactions style. For an article with final volume, issue, pages, and publication month, include those fields and omit the DOI. Include a DOI only for early-access, accepted, preprint, or otherwise not-yet-assigned final publication metadata. Verify author order, title, venue, year, volume, issue, pages, and early-access status from the primary source or reliable metadata before finalizing the list.

Use natural Chinese technical prose. Define every term and symbol before first use, explain each equation immediately after it appears, and keep the order `objects -> state/process -> rates or transitions -> constraints -> objective -> method`. Avoid unexplained abbreviations, literal translations, internal workflow language, repeated `不是……而是……` constructions, and slogans such as “全球新颖性” or “最小创新主张”.

## Idea quality requirements

The main contribution must come from a meaningful operational tension, such as:

- a new task or service lifecycle;
- persistent energy or battery constraints;
- service continuity or state handover;
- mobility, uncertainty, safety, or reliability;
- a new coupling between computation, communication, trajectory, storage, or control timescales;
- a realistic information limitation or partial-observability condition.

The following are insufficient by themselves:

- replacing DQN, PPO, SAC, MAPPO, or another algorithm;
- adding an arbitrary weighted objective to a familiar trajectory/offloading problem;
- stacking RIS, NOMA, ISAC, digital twins, blockchain, LLMs, or similar labels without operational necessity;
- claiming generic performance improvement without a new falsifiable tension;
- tuning parameters in one static simulation setting.

For every important assumption, state why it is needed, what it simplifies, how it differs from reality, and how it could later be relaxed or validated.

## Model closure checklist

Before finalizing the document, verify that:

1. Every object, set, index, timeslot, and unit is defined before first use.
2. Task arrivals, input size, computational intensity, state, and deadlines are defined where relevant.
3. Communication rates and channel assumptions match the proposed scenario.
4. Local, UAV, and other edge computation processes are defined where relevant.
5. Propulsion, transmission, computation, and migration energy are not double-counted.
6. Delay, queue, reliability, and service-continuity quantities have consistent definitions.
7. Every decision variable appears in the objective or a constraint.
8. Every objective term is computable from defined variables and states.
9. The constraint set is not obviously contradictory.
10. At least one feasible baseline policy can be constructed.
11. For stochastic, robust, online, or partially observable models, uncertainty and available information are explicit.

## Algorithm design checklist

Explain:

- the formal problem class and the couplings that make it difficult;
- which parts admit analytical treatment, decomposition, relaxation, approximation, or robust optimization;
- which parts genuinely require online learning or adaptive control;
- how discrete and continuous decisions are handled;
- how feasibility is enforced beyond reward penalties;
- where training data or online interactions come from;
- where training and online execution occur;
- what convergence, complexity, approximation, or performance analysis is realistic;
- why the proposed method is preferable to both pure optimization and end-to-end DRL for this problem.
